# Game plan: a decade of privateer rallying

Oct 13, 2026 · decided in a question-by-question session with Adrian. This plan builds on
`Rally Survival Co-op – Game Concept.md` and changes it where noted. `QUESTS.md` covers the rally
weekend in detail and fits into milestone 2 below.

## What the game is

**A group of friends start a rally team in 1990s Slovakia. On weekdays they run a backyard workshop to
pay for it; at weekends they rally. Over a decade they climb from the regional cup to an international
rally, and the world changes around them.**

Inspired by My Summer Car, but not a copy of it. What makes it our own:

1. **A rally career with friends.** Two in the car, up to two more in support. MSC is a solo life sim
   with a rally on the side; here the rally drives everything.
2. **A decade that moves.** 1996 to about 2004: new cars arrive, mobile phones turn up, prices change,
   the village changes. Not one frozen summer.
3. **A workshop business.** Fixing villagers' cars and trading parts pays for the rally car. It uses
   the same parts, fixings and tools as the rally car.
4. **The Slovak 90s.** Privatisation, closed factories, grey business and the start of a new country.

Tone: gritty, with dry humour that comes from situations and people, not jokes. Hardcore but fair.

## Story

**Premise.** 1996. The local machine factory is privatised and shut down, and a group of friends lose
their jobs there. They rent the abandoned workshop of the old farm co-op, buy a written-off car with a
loan from the businessman who bought the factory, and enter the regional cup.

- **The businessman** is the lender (repayments are the early pressure) and sponsors a rival team.
- **The arc:** regional cup, then the national championship, then events abroad (Czechia, Poland,
  Hungary, Austria and further), ending with one big international rally around 2004, the year
  Slovakia joins the EU.
- **The ending:** how the team does in the final rally (win, finish, retire) and what it did on the way
  (debt paid, deals taken) shape an epilogue. After the credits the world stays playable.

## World

- **Home:** a fictional region of Slovakia. A small open map: the village, the workshop, a shop, a post
  office, a scrapyard, a pub, and the roads out to the local stages (the Lipova map grows into this).
- **Rally events away from home:** each event has its own map, built long and narrow: the stages with
  checkpoints and service points, villages along the route, and a shop or scrapyard for emergencies.
- **Travel:** national and foreign events are reached by travelling between towns and countries: car on
  a trailer, fuel and road costs, arrival at the event's service park.
- **Language:** the world speaks Slovak (signs, newspapers, catalogue, radio, people), and Czech, Polish
  or Hungarian abroad. Menus, subtitles and the notebook are in English, with a Slovak option.
- **Time:** a game day lasts about 45 real minutes. Sleeping skips the night and saves; players can
  sleep through days they don't need ("wake me on Friday"). The rally season runs spring to autumn.
  Winter is short: mostly skipped, perhaps a few snow stages or winter jobs in the workshop.
- **The decade:** newer cars appear at the scrapyard and on the roads, mobile phones arrive around 1999
  (with poor signal in the hills), prices rise, the village changes.

## The car

### Fixings and tools

Between our current three presses per part and MSC's every bolt:

- Each part has **2-6 visible fixings**: bolts, nuts, clips, hose clamps, plugs. The player aims at each
  one with the crosshair.
- Each fixing needs **the right tool**: 10, 13 or 17 mm spanner, socket, screwdriver, pliers. Hold E
  for about half a second per fixing.
- **A fixing left loose has consequences:** the part rattles, leaks, or works itself off on a stage.
- **Order matters:** a part covering another must come off first; the head goes on the block, not
  before it.

### Engine

About 15 bolt-on modules: block, head, turbo, intercooler, intake, exhaust manifold, injectors,
alternator, starter, water pump, fan, oil pump and sump, clutch, gearbox, ECU. The block's insides stay
one sealed part.

### Tuning

- **Grades of part:** worn, stock, sport, rally (springs, dampers, brakes, turbo, exhaust, gearbox and
  so on).
- **Hand adjustments in the garage:** boost by the wastegate, ride height and damper clicks, brake bias,
  the ECU chip map. Set badly, the engine knocks or overheats.
- More power means more heat and more stress on parts, which ties tuning into damage.

### Where parts come from

- **Scrapyard wrecks:** strip used parts with the same fixings and tools. Cheap, sometimes broken.
- **Mail-order catalogue:** sport and rally parts, ordered by phone and collected at the post office
  days later.
- **Village shop:** consumables only (oil, coolant, tape, fuses, drinks, cigarettes).
- **Rally prizes and sponsors:** some parts only come from results or a sponsor's deal.

### Customising

- **Interior:** seats, steering wheel, gear knob, extra gauges, roll cage, intercom, trip meter, dash
  trinkets. Some are safety or performance parts too.
- **Paint and livery:** paint colour, panels in other colours from scrapyard cars, number boards, and
  the stickers a sponsor deal makes you carry.
- **Workshop:** tools, a lift, an engine crane, shelves, posters. Better tools make some jobs possible
  or faster.
- **Characters:** looks and clothes, team overalls, helmets, 90s tracksuits.

### Parts that don't belong

Body parts from other cars (bumper, bonnet, door, lights, mirrors) can be held on with screws, straps,
wire or tape at points the player chooses. They sit crooked, rattle, can fall off on bumps, protect less
in a crash, and fail strict scrutineering. Mechanical parts must match, or need a proper adapter part.

### Damage and wear

Damage from crashes and abuse, as built now. Wear from use and age, and rust, come later (see Later).

### The first rally car

Not decided (see Open questions). Eastern European cars first: Škoda, perhaps a Golf. Real makes and
logos need licences, so cars are clearly inspired by real ones under names of our own. Era note: the
first-generation Octavia came out in 1996, but the vRS turbo only in 2000.

## Rallies

- **Career ladder, free choice inside each tier:** regional cup, national championship, events abroad,
  the final rally. Results and a licence open the next tier. Within a tier the team picks which events
  to enter (entry fee, distance, surface) and skips the ones it can't afford.
- **Rivals:** simulated teams. They start a minute apart, as in real rallies; their times, crashes and
  retirements are simulated and shown on the results board. Their crews and cars stand in the service
  parks. Later: incidents on stages with AI cars (a crashed rival, a lost or drunk crew, a drunk local
  driving onto the stage).
- **Scrutineering:** loose at amateur level, stricter at national level, strict abroad.
- **The co-driver** works from a paper road book: tulip diagrams and distances for road sections, pace
  notes for stages ("100 left 4 tightens") generated from the route, with the team's own marks added on a
  recce, and a trip meter on the dash. They read the calls aloud over voice chat. A map is available
  early on and can be switched on as an assist. Solo drivers hear recorded calls.

## People and money

- **Team:** up to 4 players. Driver and co-driver in the rally car; players 3 and 4 run the service van
  and scout on rally days and work in the workshop on weekdays. Everything works with 1 or 2.
- **Save:** the world, workshop, cars and calendar live in the host's save. Friends keep their own
  character (looks, clothes, personal items). Solo is the same save hosted for one.
- **Saving:** when sleeping in your own bed, and automatically at rally milestones (stage finish,
  service, bivouac). Quitting elsewhere goes back to the last save.
- **Death:** the player wakes in hospital days later. The bill comes out of the team kitty, the days are
  lost, perhaps a rally is missed, and what they carried stays where they died. The others play on.
- **Money:** Slovak koruna. A physical cash box in the workshop is the team kitty (prizes, sponsors,
  workshop jobs); each player also carries pocket money. Taking from the box is visible to all.
- **Weekday work:**
  - **Customer repairs:** villagers bring their cars with faults; the team diagnoses and fixes them for
    cash and reputation. Two original customer platforms to start: a rear-engined saloon (Škoda
    120-like) and a boxy front-engined saloon (Lada-like), with many variants from trim, colour and
    fitted parts. More models arrive as the years pass.
  - **Parts trading:** strip wrecks and sell parts at the Sunday market or over the border; prices
    change from year to year.
  - **Cheating customers** (swapping their good parts for worn ones, billing work not done, bodging
    instead of fixing) is possible. Customers notice sometimes, word spreads through the village, and
    reputation drops: fewer customers, worse prices.
- **The body:** cold and wet (outside at night, in rain), and injury from hard crashes (blurred vision, a
  limp) until treated with the first-aid kit or in hospital. No hunger, thirst or tiredness meters
  (changed from the concept).
- **Alcohol and cigarettes:** drinking makes you drunk (blurred, wobbly); smoking calms you. Bribes and
  smuggling come later, together with police AI.
- **Communication:** CB radio with limited range between vehicles; mobile phones from about 1999 with
  patchy coverage.
- **On screen:** a small HUD first (prompt, tracker); moving towards almost none (gauges, cash, watch,
  paper) later.
- **Multiplayer:** private co-op and solo only. Open servers are dropped for now (changed from the
  concept).

## Build order

Each milestone ends with automated tests, as the car systems have now.

1. **Fixings and tools on the Octavia.** Fixings data per part, tool items with sizes, hold to turn,
   consequences of loose fixings, the order parts come off and go on. Convert the existing panels,
   wheels, running gear and underside. Split the engine into modules. Sport and rally grades (springs,
   brakes, turbo first) and the hand adjustments.
2. **The rally weekend.** From `QUESTS.md`: stage timing and results, service, the road book and pace
   notes, plus simulated rivals and basic scrutineering.
3. **Home and the week.** The home map (village, workshop, shop, post office, scrapyard), the day clock,
   sleep and save, the team kitty, the catalogue and post office, and the written-off car to rebuild
   at the start.
4. **The workshop business.** Customer repairs on the first customer platform, parts trading,
   reputation.
5. **Season one.** The regional cup calendar, the businessman's loan, the first story beats, the season
   end and a short winter.
6. **Body and vices.** Cold, wet, injury, hospital and death; alcohol and cigarettes.
7. **Up the ladder.** The national tier, travel and event maps, stricter scrutineering, the second
   customer platform, the service van and scout roles.
8. **The decade.** Events abroad, the era changing year by year, phones, the final rally and the
   epilogue.

- A fuller winter calendar.
- Almost no HUD.
